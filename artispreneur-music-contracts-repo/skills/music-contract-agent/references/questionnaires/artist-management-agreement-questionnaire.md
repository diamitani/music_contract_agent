# Artist Management Agreement — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/artist-management-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Artist (and loan-out entity, if any) and Manager (and management company).

## What this contract type affects

- Income/commission
- Term, options, and termination
- Approval/control
- Exclusivity

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Artist (and loan-out entity, if any) and Manager (and management company)). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Commission rate (typically 15-20% of gross income)
- What income is commissionable (recording, publishing, touring, merch, brand deals, sync)
- What is excluded (e.g., pre-existing deals, songwriting-only income if a separate publisher exists)
- Commission on income earned after termination (post-term/sunset commission) and for how long

**Rights**
- Manager is NOT granted ownership of masters or compositions — management is a service, not an ownership stake
- Power of attorney scope, if any — should be narrow (signing on artist's behalf only for specifically listed acts)

**Control & Risk**
- Exclusivity: is the Manager the artist's only manager, or only for certain territories/activities?
- Term length and whether there are renewal options tied to performance benchmarks
- Termination triggers: for cause (breach, fraud, inactivity) and for convenience (notice period)
- Key-person clause if signing with a management company, not an individual

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Red flag:** commission on ALL income for life, or with no sunset after termination.
**Red flag:** manager also acting as booking agent, label, or publisher without disclosure — commission stacking.
**Yellow:** automatic renewal options with no artist opt-out or performance trigger.
**Yellow:** broad power of attorney allowing the manager to sign contracts or spend money without artist sign-off.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- Is this a new relationship or formalizing an existing informal one? (If informal, note the actual start date — commission disputes often hinge on this.)
- What commission rate did they agree on verbally, if any?
- Is the manager an individual or does the artist need to contract with their company?
- Does the artist already have a separate publisher, booking agent, or label? (affects what's commissionable and avoids double-commissioning)
- How long should the initial term run, and should renewal depend on the manager hitting specific milestones (e.g., securing a certain amount of income)?
