# Influencer / Content Creator Agreement — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/influencer-content-creator-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Creator/Artist and Brand or Platform.

## What this contract type affects

- Content usage rights and license scope
- Exclusivity/category restrictions
- Disclosure/FTC compliance

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Creator/Artist and Brand or Platform). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Flat fee vs. performance-based (affiliate/commission) compensation
- Payment schedule tied to deliverables

**Rights**
- What usage rights does the brand get to the content — organic posting only, or paid ad usage ('whitelisting'), and for how long?
- Does the brand own the content, or just license it?

**Control & Risk**
- Number and type of deliverables (posts, stories, videos) and approval/revision process
- Required FTC disclosure language (#ad, #sponsored)
- Category exclusivity (can't promote a competing brand for X period)

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Red flag:** perpetual, unlimited paid-ad usage rights to the creator's likeness/content for a one-time flat fee — this is usually worth significantly more and should be priced separately or time-limited.
**Yellow:** no FTC disclosure requirement written into the contract — legal risk for the creator, not just the brand.
**Yellow:** broad exclusivity/non-compete with no time limit.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- What deliverables are expected (# of posts, platform, format) and by when?
- Is the brand asking to run the content as paid ads ('whitelisting')? If so, that needs separate, time-limited compensation.
- Is there a category exclusivity requirement, and for how long?
- What's the payment structure — flat fee, commission, or both?

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
