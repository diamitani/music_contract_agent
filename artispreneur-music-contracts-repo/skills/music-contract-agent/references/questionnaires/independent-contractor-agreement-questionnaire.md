# Independent Contractor Agreement (General Services) — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/independent-contractor-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Client (hiring party) and Contractor (service provider).

## What this contract type affects

- Scope of services
- Ownership of deliverables (work-for-hire vs. licensed use)
- Confidentiality

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Client (hiring party) and Contractor (service provider)). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Flat fee, retainer, or per-session rate
- Payment schedule and reimbursable-expense pre-approval

**Rights**
- This is the catch-all template — use it for PR, marketing, social media management, or single-date session work that doesn't have its own dedicated template. The single most important decision is Section 5: work-for-hire (client owns everything) vs. licensed use (contractor keeps ownership, client gets a license).

**Control & Risk**
- Deliverables, deadlines, and included revision rounds
- Term and notice period to terminate
- Whether the contractor is free to work with other clients (should be yes, by default, unless real exclusivity is negotiated and paid for)

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Always clarify Section 5 explicitly before finalizing** — a contractor should never end up in a work-for-hire arrangement by default without understanding they're giving up all future rights to what they create.
**Yellow:** independent contractor status claimed but the actual working relationship looks like employment (set hours, exclusive availability, day-to-day supervision) — this can create real legal risk for the hiring party depending on jurisdiction; flag it rather than silently drafting around it.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- What specific services, deliverables, and deadlines are involved?
- What's the fee structure — flat fee, retainer, or per-session?
- Should the deliverables be a full work-for-hire buyout, or does the contractor keep ownership and license use to the client?
- Is any exclusivity actually intended, or is the contractor free to work with others?

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
