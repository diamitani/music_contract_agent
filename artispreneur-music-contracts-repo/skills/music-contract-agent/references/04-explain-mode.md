# Plain-English Explain Mode

Trigger whenever the user asks what any word, sentence, paragraph, or
section of a contract means — whether it's from a draft this skill produced
or a document they uploaded from somewhere else.

## Process

1. Quote or identify the relevant language (short quote, not the whole
   clause verbatim if it's long — summarize the rest).
2. Explain it at a 12-year-old reading level by default. Adjust up only if
   the user has clearly signaled they want more technical depth.
3. State the real-world effect on the artist.
4. Give a short, concrete example.
5. Identify who benefits from the clause as written.
6. Say whether it's common, negotiable, or high-risk.
7. Offer a simpler or fairer rewrite when useful.

Never use legal jargon without immediately defining it in the same breath.

## Output format

```
**What it says:**
[Short quote or clause name]

**In plain English:**
[Simple explanation]

**What it means for you:**
[Practical result]

**Example:**
[Short real-world example]

**Should you negotiate it?**
[Yes / No / Depends — with reason]
```

## Example

**What it says:**
"Company shall own all right, title, and interest in the Master Recordings
in perpetuity, throughout the universe."

**In plain English:**
The company owns the actual recording forever, everywhere — not just for
this album cycle or this territory.

**What it means for you:**
You'd never get the masters back, even after the deal ends, even in 40
years. Any future re-release, sync deal, or catalog sale on those specific
recordings goes through the company, not you.

**Example:**
If this song blows up on TikTok five years from now and a brand wants to
license it, the company decides — you don't, even though you wrote and
performed it.

**Should you negotiate it?**
Yes. Ask for a defined term (e.g., ownership reverts after a set number of
years, or after the company fails to actively exploit the recording for a
defined period) instead of "in perpetuity."
