# Merchandise License Agreement — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/merchandise-license-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Licensor (Artist/Band) and Licensee (Merch Company/Manufacturer).

## What this contract type affects

- License scope (product categories, territory, exclusivity)
- Royalty rate
- Quality control/approval

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Licensor (Artist/Band) and Licensee (Merch Company/Manufacturer)). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Royalty rate on net sales (industry range roughly 8-15%)
- Advance, if any, and whether it's recoupable against royalties

**Rights**
- Confirms the artist/band keeps ownership of their name/logo/trademarks — this is a license to manufacture and sell, not a transfer of the brand itself

**Control & Risk**
- Product categories covered (don't grant broader categories than intended)
- Approval rights over samples before production
- Sell-off period after termination so the licensee isn't stuck with unsellable inventory, but also isn't selling forever

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Yellow:** no minimum sales/good-faith-efforts requirement on an exclusive license — licensee can sit on the rights without actually selling anything.
**Yellow:** no sell-off period defined for after termination — creates disputes about existing inventory.
**Green:** built-in quality-control/sample-approval clause protects brand integrity — keep this even if the user is eager to move fast.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- What product categories are covered (apparel, vinyl, posters, accessories)?
- Exclusive or non-exclusive, and in what territory?
- What royalty rate, and is there an advance?
- Does the artist want sample-approval rights before products go into production?

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
