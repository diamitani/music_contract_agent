# LLC Operating Agreement (Music Venture) — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/llc-operating-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Members (co-founders of the label/venture).

## What this contract type affects

- Ownership percentages (membership interest)
- Management structure (member-managed vs. manager-managed)
- Profit/loss distribution

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Members (co-founders of the label/venture)). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Capital contributions per member
- Distribution schedule and priority (return of capital before profit split, etc.)

**Rights**
- Defines company-level ownership of the business itself (and by extension, any IP the LLC holds), separate from any individual artist's personal masters/publishing — clarify what assets actually sit inside the LLC

**Control & Risk**
- Voting rights and what requires unanimous vs. majority approval
- Buy-sell provisions: what happens if a member wants to leave, dies, or wants to sell their stake
- Admission of new members

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Always recommend attorney review** for LLC formation documents — state-specific filing requirements and tax elections matter and are outside this skill's scope.
**Yellow:** no buy-sell/exit provision — can trap members in a business relationship with no clean way out.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- How many members, and what percentage does each hold?
- Is this member-managed (all members vote on decisions) or manager-managed (one or a few run day-to-day)?
- What happens if a member wants to exit, stops contributing, or the members disagree on a major decision?
- What state will the LLC be formed in? (This determines filing requirements outside this document — recommend confirming with a local attorney or formation service.)

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
