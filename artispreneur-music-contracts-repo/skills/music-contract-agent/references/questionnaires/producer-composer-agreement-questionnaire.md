# Producer / Composer Agreement — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/producer-composer-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Producer/Composer and Artist or Commissioning Party.

## What this contract type affects

- Master ownership vs. work-for-hire
- Composer credit and royalty points
- Delivery and usage scope

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Producer/Composer and Artist or Commissioning Party). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Flat fee vs. royalty points (or both)
- Payment schedule (deposit, on-delivery, on-release)
- Whether points are paid on net or gross, and from what revenue streams (streaming, sync, physical)

**Rights**
- Is this work-for-hire (commissioning party owns everything) or does the producer/composer retain a royalty/points interest and credit?
- Sample/loop clearance responsibility — who warrants the material is clean

**Control & Risk**
- Number of revisions/rounds included in the fee before extra charges apply
- Delivery format and deadline
- Credit requirements (liner notes, streaming metadata, video credits)

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Yellow:** 'full buyout, no further compensation' language when the producer expected points — clarify before signing.
**Yellow:** no defined revision limit, opening the door to unlimited free rework.
**Red flag:** producer required to warrant sample clearance without being told what samples were used.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- Is this a flat-fee work-for-hire, or does the producer/composer keep a royalty/points interest?
- What's the fee, and what payment milestones trigger payment?
- How many revision rounds are included before additional charges apply?
- What credit does the producer/composer receive, and where (streaming, liner notes, video)?

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
