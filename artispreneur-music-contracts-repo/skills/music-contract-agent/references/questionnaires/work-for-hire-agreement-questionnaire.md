# Work-for-Hire Agreement — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/work-for-hire-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Hiring Party and Contractor (writer/producer/engineer/designer/etc.).

## What this contract type affects

- Full ownership transfer of the work product to the hiring party
- Compensation for that transfer
- Warranty of originality

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Hiring Party and Contractor (writer/producer/engineer/designer/etc.)). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Flat fee (work-for-hire is almost always a one-time buyout, not royalty-bearing)
- Payment schedule tied to delivery/milestones

**Rights**
- Confirms complete transfer of copyright to the hiring party — the contractor keeps nothing unless a royalty/credit is separately negotiated
- This is the highest-risk-of-regret template for the contractor — make sure they understand it's a full buyout before they sign as the one giving up rights

**Control & Risk**
- Delivery deadline and format
- Revision rounds included
- Credit, if any (work-for-hire doesn't guarantee credit unless stated)

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Always flag for attorney review** when a substantial or potentially high-value work is being fully bought out for a flat fee — make sure the contractor actually intends to give up all future royalty/backend interest.
**Yellow:** no credit provision even though the contractor expected one — add explicit credit language if desired.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- Which side is the user on — the hiring party or the person being hired?
- What's being created, and what's the flat fee?
- Does the contractor want any credit, even though this is a full buyout?
- Is the contractor fully aware this gives up all future royalty/ownership interest? (if unsure, flag for attorney review before finalizing)

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
