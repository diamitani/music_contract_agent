# Non-Exclusive Beat License Agreement — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/non-exclusive-beat-license-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Producer (beat creator) and Artist (licensee).

## What this contract type affects

- Usage limitations (streams, videos, no broadcast/sync)
- Producer credit
- No ownership transfer

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Producer (beat creator) and Artist (licensee)). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- License fee (typically the lowest tier a producer sells)

**Rights**
- This is a LICENSE, not ownership — the producer can and will sell the same beat to other artists. Make sure the user understands that before they build a whole campaign around a beat they don't have exclusive rights to.

**Control & Risk**
- Usage caps (stream/download limits, no broadcast/sync/paid ads without upgrading)
- Upgrade path to exclusive if the song starts to take off

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Always flag:** if the artist's song starts getting real traction, this license's usage caps can become a real problem (e.g., it wasn't licensed for broadcast or paid ads). Recommend upgrading to an exclusive license before the song is pushed commercially at scale.
**Green:** clear, low-cost way to test a beat before committing to an exclusive purchase — appropriate default for early-stage work.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- What's the license fee, and what usage tier (streams, video use, no broadcast) is being purchased?
- Does the artist plan to push this song commercially at scale? If so, discuss upgrading to exclusive before finalizing a release plan.

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
