# Exclusive Beat License Agreement — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/exclusive-beat-license-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Producer (beat creator) and Artist (purchaser).

## What this contract type affects

- Exclusivity of the beat
- Ownership of the underlying beat vs. the finished song
- Publishing split on the new recording

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Producer (beat creator) and Artist (purchaser)). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Purchase price and payment schedule (deposit vs. full payment)
- Whether a separate publishing split applies on top of the purchase price

**Rights**
- Clarify up front: is this an exclusive LICENSE (producer keeps underlying copyright, artist can't resell the beat itself) or a full BUYOUT (producer assigns copyright entirely)? These are priced very differently and the template defaults to license unless Section 4(c) is activated.
- Confirm whether the beat is being removed from beat-store/marketplace listings once paid — this is what 'exclusive' actually means operationally, not just legally

**Control & Risk**
- Reversion window if the artist never releases anything using the beat
- Producer credit requirement and exact tag wording

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Always confirm with the producer:** are they aware they're giving up the right to sell this beat to anyone else, permanently, for this price? Producers sometimes under-price exclusives without registering what they're giving up.
**Yellow:** no reversion clause — if the artist never uses the beat, the producer is locked out of ever selling it again with no time limit.
**Yellow:** publishing split percentage left blank — this is a real revenue stream, not boilerplate; don't let it default silently.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- Is this a full buyout (producer keeps nothing) or an exclusive license (producer keeps underlying ownership but can't sell to anyone else)?
- What's the purchase price, and is it paid upfront or as a deposit + balance on delivery?
- What publishing percentage does the producer keep on the finished song?
- Is there a deadline by which the artist needs to release something, after which the producer can take the beat back?

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
