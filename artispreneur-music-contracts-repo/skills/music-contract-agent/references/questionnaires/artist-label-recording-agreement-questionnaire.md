# Artist / Label Recording Agreement — How to Use This Template & Questionnaire

Source template: `references/templates/artist-label-recording-agreement.md`

## When to use this document

Use this agreement when the deal involves: Artist and Label.

## What this contract type affects

- Master recording ownership
- Royalty rate and recoupment
- Term/album commitment
- Creative approvals

## Step 1 — Find out who's asking

Confirm which side of the deal the user represents (Artist and Label). Artist-protective
defaults in this skill are written from the perspective of the independent artist/
creator side of the deal. If the user is the other party (the hiring/paying side),
say so plainly and adjust: still be fair and accurate, but don't silently apply
artist-favoring defaults to someone who isn't the artist without telling them.

## Step 2 — Ask only what's missing

Use these grouped questions. Skip anything already answered in the conversation,
uploaded files, or pasted messages.

**Parties**
- Full legal names of all parties (and stage/business names)
- Notice addresses/emails
- Entity/signatory authority, if a company or LLC is involved

**Project & Money**
- Advance amount and recoupment structure
- Royalty rate (% of net receipts) and how it's calculated
- Recording fund / budget and whether unspent funds go to the artist
- Whether costs (video, marketing, tour support) are recoupable or cross-collateralized against other income

**Rights**
- Who owns the masters — Label (traditional deal), Artist with a license to Label (artist-friendly/distribution deal), or a joint venture
- Territory and format (all media now known or later devised is broad — flag it)
- Whether rights are exclusive to this label or the artist can also self-release other work

**Control & Risk**
- Album/song delivery commitment (how many records, minimum length/quality standard)
- Creative control over artwork, singles, mixing/mastering, release timing
- Options: does the label have the right to pick up further albums, and on what terms?
- Term and what happens if the label doesn't release the record within X months (reversion)

## Step 3 — Watch for these risk patterns

**Red flag:** perpetual or 'life of copyright' master ownership transfer with no reversion.
**Red flag:** cross-collateralization across unrelated projects (video costs recouped from touring, etc.).
**Yellow:** vague 'all rights in all media now known or hereafter devised, throughout the universe' language — push for defined scope.
**Yellow:** open-ended multi-album options with no minimum advance guaranteed for later albums.

## Contract-specific questions to ask

- Does the artist want to keep master ownership (distribution-style deal) or is a traditional ownership transfer acceptable given the advance/support offered?
- What advance, if any, is on the table, and what specifically does it need to cover?
- How many albums/songs is this deal for, and is there an option for more?
- What happens if the label doesn't release the music — is there a release deadline with reversion rights?
- What creative approvals does the artist want to keep (single choice, artwork, video treatment)?

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] All placeholders replaced (no blank underscores or `[BRACKETS]` left in the final draft)
- [ ] Money terms consistent everywhere they appear in the document
- [ ] Rights grant does not contradict the ownership section
- [ ] Term/termination/renewal dates don't conflict
- [ ] Signature blocks match the named parties exactly
- [ ] Attorney-review notice included per `references/05-safety-and-legal-boundaries.md`
- [ ] If any risk flag above is Red, the attorney-review recommendation is called out explicitly in the delivery, not buried in the fine print
